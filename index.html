Fridge Pickles v 1.1

Weigh your cucumbers, estimate how much brine will fit around them, and scale the whole-spice mix to suit.

Fridge only. Keep at 4 °C or colder and use within 2 weeks of making. This recipe is not shelf stable.

Measure whole seeds and peppercorns with a level measuring spoon. 1 tsp = 5 mL. Simple eighth-teaspoon fractions are shown where they fit the batch; other amounts use decimal teaspoons. Cinnamon means a cut or snapped length of quill. For fractional berries, cloves or leaves, break them up or round to taste.

Pack and refrigerate

  1. Wash and trim the cucumbers, then slice or cut into spears. Divide the spices among clean, heatproof jars and pack the cucumbers snugly.
  2. Combine food-grade 8% vinegar, water, preserving salt and optional sugar. If using food-grade calcium chloride flakes, stir in the measured dose. Bring to a boil for 2 minutes.
  3. Pour the hot brine over the cucumbers to cover, leaving about 1.3 cm headspace. Lid the jars and refrigerate promptly at 4 °C or colder. Allow 1–2 days for flavour to develop. Use within 2 weeks of making.
  4. If you need more brine, make a fresh portion at the same ratio. For 100 mL, use 31.3 mL of 8% vinegar, 68.7 mL water, 3 mL preserving salt and, if using sugar, 0.8 g. If using the flakes, scale them at the same rate of 0.625 mL per litre. Bring it to a boil for 2 minutes before adding.

How the estimate works

The default assumes 600 g of cucumber fills 1 L of nominal jar capacity and 1 g displaces about 1 mL. That leaves an estimated 400 mL of brine space per litre of packed jars. The calculator adds 15% by default rounds that volume up to the next 10 mL, then increases it by 50%.

Jar count rounds up so you have enough containers. The estimate only counts the space around the cucumbers you pack, so it allows for a partly filled final jar. Headspace and jar shape are approximate. Cut, packing density and evaporation can change how much brine you need. Keep a smaller jar handy for the remainder, and make extra brine if needed.

Australian metric measures use a 5 mL teaspoon and a 20 mL tablespoon. Use food-grade Artisans Bottega calcium chloride flakes marked 77% CaCl₂ (E509). This recipe uses half the stated pack dose, or 0.625 mL per litre of brine.
